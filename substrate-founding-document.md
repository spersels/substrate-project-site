# The Substrate Project — Founding Document

*A designed foundation for machine-authored software*

**Status:** v0.9 — public edition
**Date:** August 2026 · *The working repository retains the full document history and version files.*

---

## 1. Premise

**The context, before the premise: the authority transfer.** The rise of AI is not a future to prepare for; it is a transfer already in motion. Humanity is granting AI systems authority to act in the world — incrementally, voluntarily, and mostly without noticing the aggregate: from *advising* (suggestions, completions), to *acting under review* (agents with a human gate), to *acting autonomously* (agents that send the message, move the money, operate the machine). Each grant is individually rational — a product decision, a productivity gain. Collectively they constitute the largest transfer of operational authority in human history, conducted so far without an architecture of accountability. No one decided this; it emerges from a million reasonable decisions — the same shape as every problem in this document.

The transfer creates a gap no existing institution covers. Everything humanity uses to govern action — law, liability, audit, deterrence — assumes actors that are *persons*: attributable, accountable, slow, mortal, ownable of assets, jailable. AI agents break every assumption at once: they act at machine speed and scale, are copied rather than counted, hold nothing that can be seized and serve sentences nowhere, and their decisions are stochastic computation rather than examinable testimony. Authority is migrating onto actors on which the governance stack has no grip surface. The requirement this creates is domain-independent: an infrastructure of **authorized action** — every consequential AI act *attributable* (which agent), *authorized* (on whose behalf, with what right), *bounded* (within what scope), and *accountable* (consequences binding an identifiable principal). Humanity will need this wherever agents act: finance, communications, physical systems, government.

This document is about one arena — software — for three reasons that make it the right one to build first. It is where the transfer is **furthest advanced**: machine authorship of code is already the norm at the margin. It is the **medium of every other transfer**: an AI agent acts on the world *through code*, so software is the actuation layer of everything digital, and whoever solves authorized action for software has built the enforcement substrate every other domain needs. And it is where the problem is **most tractable**: code is formalizable, its rails are digital, and the verification tradition, capability discipline, and cryptographic infrastructure already exist. Software is the beachhead, not the boundary — the schema built here (evidence, capabilities, mandates, provenance) is designed as the general pattern, and §9 carries it outward as far as robot actuators. Within that arena, the premise:

When code generation becomes nearly free, verification becomes the bottleneck. Nearly everything programming languages have been optimized for since Fortran — human readability, ergonomics, terseness, forgiving semantics — depreciates as the author becomes a machine. What appreciates is everything that makes code **checkable**.

The scarce resource in an era of machine authorship is not code; it is *justified confidence* that code is correct and contained. This project designs the substrate for that era deliberately, rather than letting it be inherited by accident from languages designed for human fingers.

**The failure mode has a name.** Left to defaults, the era ends in the **Eloi Phenomenon** — the progressive, comfortable, compounding loss of a civilization's capacity to understand its own operational infrastructure: systems that keep working while the *why* disappears, arriving not by catastrophe but by convenience, one rational decision at a time. The mechanism (provenance decay, the last-reader clock), the measurements (the dark code fraction, the cause-unknown rate), and the registered trajectory are carried by the companion problem statement, ***Root Cause: Unknown*** — kept deliberately solution-free, because problems recruit and solutions polarize. This document is the solution side: everything that follows is the design of the alternative — an era in which the why is *kept*, structurally, by construction.

**"Correct" is not one property.** The word must always be anchored — correct *relative to what* — and the project distinguishes four layers with opposite economics under machine authorship:

- **Layer 1 — Soundness:** it doesn't break. No crashes, undefined behavior, capability escapes, or data races. Delivered *by construction* by the substrate itself; requires no spec.
- **Layer 2 — Conformance:** it does what the spec says. The proof-carrying story: machine implementation, machine proof, machine checker. Fully automatable in principle.
- **Layer 3 — Fidelity:** the spec says what the requirements meant. The requirement→spec translation is a natural-language-to-formal judgment; a machine-checked proof anchored to a mistranslated spec *launders* the mistranslation.
- **Layer 4 — Fit:** the requirements capture what stakeholders actually need. Requirements are a lossy projection of intent, and intent moves. No formal method touches this layer.

Machine authorship makes layers 1–2 radically cheaper — that is the thesis — while making layers 3–4 *more* dangerous: generation volume multiplies requirement→spec translations (increasingly performed by models interpreting prose), and layer 1–2 rigor casts a halo of false confidence over them. "Proven correct" is the most misleading phrase in the stack when the proof is anchored to the wrong spec. The substrate's obligation is to make the anchor explicit, recorded, and queryable (§3.6).

**The trust boundary, stated once.** Trust in what code *does* (layers 1–2) must be structural and mathematical — no stochastic substitute is acceptable at any model capability, ever. Trust in what code is *for* (layers 3–4) cannot be formal — by any method, ever. The entire design problem is making the boundary between those two regions explicit, minimal, and impossible to cross silently (§3.1).

**Where the bottleneck actually binds: reversibility.** For reversible actions with contained blast radius, ex-post trust — canaries, observability, rollback — is economically unbeatable and will remain so; the thesis does not contest that ground. Verification binds where actions are *irreversible* — money moved, messages sent, contracts signed, actuators actuated — where trust must exist before execution because there is nothing to roll back. Machine agency is what makes this decisive: a human-written application mostly manipulates its own state; an agent with tools acts on the world, and every tool handed to an agent converts reversible software into irreversible action. The claim is therefore not "verification becomes the bottleneck for software"; it is: **verification binds on the irreversible frontier, and the frontier is expanding.** Narrower than the v0.1 formulation, stronger for it, and it comes with a buyer who exists today.

**The thesis at calibrated confidence.** (a) Machine agents acting irreversibly need trust that is pre-execution, composable, and auditable, and stochastic assurance categorically cannot supply it — *high confidence; the part to bet on.* (b) Whether that evidence layer works dramatically better as a purpose-built substrate than as tooling on existing stacks is an *open empirical question* — Phase 0 exists to answer it cheaply before anything is built. (c) Risk concentrates permanently at the top of the tower — fidelity, gaming, ceremonial ratification — *high confidence about the risk; low confidence that anyone, this project included, yet has the containment story.*

## 2. The Design Hierarchy

Properties of the substrate, in order of importance:

**2.1 Capability security as the keystone.** Ambient authority is eliminated from the language itself: a module cannot express a network call, file write, or database access unless explicitly handed that capability as a value. This is the load-bearing property because it is the only one that *degrades gracefully under partial verification* — proofs tell you a component is correct; capabilities bound the cost when it isn't. It contains the unverified majority of any real system and survives the FFI boundary where verification-maximalism dies. (Lineage: E, Pony, CHERI, the WASM component model, Pel's grammar-level capability control.)

**2.2 Checkability at every layer.** Rich static types, effect systems that declare everything a function can do, refinement types, contracts, and — where stakes justify it — proof-carrying code. The trust model: humans read specs; machines generate implementations and machine-checkable proofs; checkers, not reviewers, establish correctness. This is the only trust model that scales with generation volume.

**2.3 Locality of reasoning — for the verifier.** A function's signature tells you everything: no hidden mutation, no action through globals. Verification cost scales with what must be inspected to trust a change; locality makes diffs verifiable in isolation and lets many agents work in parallel without interference. Locality is a concurrency property of the development process, and compositionality is what lets proof cost scale sub-linearly.

**2.4 Programs as semantic structures, not text.** Edits are typed, verifiable operations ("rename," "extract," "strengthen this precondition"), not textual patches. Merge conflicts become semantic questions with decidable answers.

**2.5 Determinism and hermeticity.** Agent workflows are generate → run → observe → repair loops; nondeterminism poisons the signal. Reproducible builds and execution make the loop converge. Compiler speed and error-message quality are inference-loop properties — an error message is effectively the next prompt. The same properties are the *forensic* infrastructure: deterministic replay converts an AI assistant's incident-time claims from testimony into demonstrations.

**What matters less than commonly assumed:** token-efficient syntax (verbosity costs shrink with context and constrained decoding) and the training-corpus moat (in-context learning from spec + examples shrinks the cold-start penalty — which is what makes this design space viable at all).

## 3. The Trust Model and Its Honest Limits

**3.1 The deterministic terminus: stochastic authorship, deterministic accountability.** The axiom everything else in this section presupposes. A stochastic reviewer cannot produce justified confidence at any capability level, for reasons that are structural rather than facts about current model quality:

- **Its approval is testimony, not evidence.** It does not compose, does not transfer between consumers, and does not cache — "model M glanced at it" has the same epistemic type as "a reviewer glanced at it." The trust has been relocated, not discharged.
- **Its failures are correlated with the author's.** When the same class of system writes and reviews the code, the reviewer shares the author's blind spots by construction — and scale deepens the correlation rather than diluting it.
- **Its confidence expires on contact with change.** Stochastic assurance is re-purchased on every diff forever — a permanent operating expense, which is exactly the cost structure §2.2 says cannot scale. Deterministic evidence is the only kind that accumulates (§4, content addressing).

Therefore every trust chain must terminate deterministically — the de Bruijn criterion: a small checker whose verdict is reproducible and whose code is read with total rigor. **Never trust the author; trust the certificate.** This is what converts ever-larger stochastic models from a threat into an asset: they serve as provers, searchers, and generators — the more capable the better — while nothing they emit is trusted until it survives the checker. Generation may be arbitrarily unreliable if verification is sober; producing is hard, checking is mechanical, and that asymmetry is the one place in the design space where the mathematics is unambiguously favorable.

Three limits keep the axiom from collapsing into verification-absolutism:

- **The trusted base never reaches zero.** The checker, the normalization function, the hardware beneath them — every proof bottoms out in artifacts vetted by reading, testing, and social process, not by proof. The achievable goal is a trusted base small enough to read with total rigor (kilobytes, not gigabytes): a reduction of many orders of magnitude, not an elimination.
- **A proof is only as good as its anchor.** Formal rigor below the spec cannot repair — and actively launders — a judgment error above it (§1 layers 3–4, §3.6). Demanding "absolutely formal trust" of the whole stack either quietly redefines trust as mere conformance, or pretends the anchor is formal when it is not.
- **Reproducible empirical evidence is legitimate.** Hermetic N-version cross-checking and property-based testing are deterministic in the sense that matters: anyone re-running them gets the same verdict. Stratified verification (§3.3) is this axiom applied economically, not a compromise of it.

A fourth limit is the sharpest, and it is a wound the design must carry openly: **correlation migrates upward.** If models author the specs — and at volume they will — the deterministic proof chain hangs from stochastic anchors, and the correlation argument above applies *at the anchor*: the trust is not discharged, it moves to the layer with the least tooling. Only partial answers exist, and they must be built in rather than hoped for: **diversity at the anchor** (different model families for spec authorship, implementation, and adversarial spec-audit, breaking the shared-blind-spot structure) and **human attention spent adversarially** — not universal review, which the volume premise forbids, but audit-sampling of high-stakes anchors, the way tax authorities audit returns. Trust at the top of the tower is statistical and adversarial, never universal. Weaker than one would wish; recorded as such.

The axiom guards against twin failure modes, and both must be named: the **accident-substrate**, where civilization's operational trust is "a model glanced at it" (§8); and the **formal halo**, where a stack so impressively proven makes everyone forget that the anchor was a judgment call made by an agent interpreting prose. The deterministic terminus answers the first; recorded, attributed judgment (§3.6, §3.9) answers the second. Neither answer substitutes for the other.

**3.2 The spec is where difficulty relocates.** For algorithmic kernels (crypto, compilers, protocols), specs compress and the pure model works. For the majority of software — glue, product logic — the spec is roughly as long and bug-prone as the code. Worse: when the same class of system writes both implementation and proof, underspecification plus an optimizing agent yields *specification gaming with a machine-checked certificate*. The human burden becomes adversarial spec auditing.

**3.3 Stratified verification is the design, not a compromise.** The realistic equilibrium: a proof-carrying core (authorization, money movement, concurrency primitives, parsers), a vast empirically-validated periphery, and capability boundaries determining blast radius of the unverified mass. Cheap generation also makes *empirical* confidence cheap — N-version generation with massive property-based cross-checking is now economically viable and handles spec churn better than proof maintenance does.

**3.4 Readability becomes projection.** Human-readable views must be **deterministic, verified projections** of the canonical structure (a proven pretty-printer), never LLM paraphrase — an inference-based translator duplicates the trust problem with correlated failures, and fails worst exactly on the off-distribution code where scrutiny matters most. The projection layer must also cover *runtime* behavior: traces and failures mapped back to spec-level terms (the source-level-debugger lesson from the compiler era). This is a forced move, not a preference: under machine authorship, readability is exiting the canonical artifact regardless (§8 — the three locks and the machine-efficiency drift); the verified projection layer is the only mechanism by which it survives at all.

The stance, stated once: **unread is inevitable; unaccountable is a choice.** Reading-by-default ends with human authorship, and the project does not mourn it or resist it — reading was always a *proxy* for justified confidence, and a poor one (slow, non-composing, non-caching, empirically weak). What must survive is reading-**on-demand**: the regenerable, verified account, summonable at incident time. Machine authorship inverts the old economics — under human authorship, reading was cheap and evidence expensive; now evidence is cheap (the prover is free, §3.1) and reading becomes the scarce act, to be *spent* adversarially where it matters most (§3.5). The substrate's position against the "humans back in the loop" reflex follows directly: let the machines write everything, unread, at full speed — and make the evidence mandatory instead of the eyeballs. A toll gate manned by a sleepy guard resists throughput; a transponder requirement instruments it.

**3.5 The human boundary.** Humans read a small, adversarially-selected fraction of code — the fraction implicated in incidents, exploits, audits. Trust compresses into a small trusted base that must be read with total rigor: the specs, the checker, the capability policy, and the projection function itself. The human role at incident time is a computationally weak verifier interrogating a powerful untrusted oracle: every AI-assistant claim must bottom out in something mechanically checkable (replayable traces, capability policy lookups). The irreducible human task is judgment about *intent* — whether the spec should have permitted what it provably permitted. Key operational metric: **mean time to understanding (MTTU)** — how fast a human under pressure gets from "something's wrong" to a correct spec-level account of why.

**3.6 Fidelity: requirements as first-class nodes.** Verification (built the thing right, layers 1–2) must not be conflated with validation (built the right thing, layers 3–4). Layer 3 cannot be mechanized, but it can be *contained* by refusing to let the requirement→spec translation happen invisibly. Requirements enter the semantic store as first-class, versioned, content-addressed nodes; every spec node cites the requirement nodes it claims to discharge. Fidelity then becomes auditable even where it cannot be provable:

- **Coverage queries fall out for free:** which requirements trace to no spec (gaps); which definitions trace to no requirement (rogue or dead functionality — an underrated security signal); which requirements changed since their specs were last reviewed (staleness).
- **The spec audit is bidirectional:** downward (is the spec internally coherent, are its edge cases intended) and upward (does it faithfully render requirement R). The human burden of §3.2 is properly both directions.
- **MTTU extends through the full chain:** incident forensics must end not at "the spec permitted it" but at "requirement R, translated by agent A on date D, is where intent diverged."

The requirement→spec link remains the unformalizable boundary where formal artifacts meet human purposes (§3.5). The substrate cannot mechanize that judgment; it can force the judgment to be recorded, attributed, and queryable.

One honesty clause, learned from forty years of traceability practice: recorded links get **gamed**. Hollow citations that satisfy the letter of coverage are the *default outcome to defeat*, not an edge case — every prior traceability regime (DO-178, CMMI) degenerated into compliance theater because the links were maintained by humans at high cost for no consumer but auditors. Machine-maintained links with automated consumers — coverage queries that gate deployment, drift checks that reopen elicitation — change the economics that produced the theater; whether they change the *outcome* is precisely what H6 and H7 exist to test. Until they report, the defensible claim for this section is **visibility, not containment**.

**3.7 The recursion upward: intent as structure.** The same move that demoted code-as-text applies to requirements-as-text. Prose requirements are documents standing in for what is actually a graph of goals, constraints, priorities, and tradeoff decisions. The canonical requirement is a typed semantic object in the same store — *maintain property P over state S, with priority ordering G₁ > G₂ over conflicting goals, ratified by principal X on date D* — and prose is a projection of it. The tower unifies: **mandate → goals → requirements → specs → code → runtime traces**, one continuous content-addressed structure, with checkable transformations between adjacent layers where possible and recorded judgments where not.

**3.8 Elicitation as compilation.** A requirement-elicitation conversation is active learning against the human: the AI maintains a distribution over candidate formal intents and asks the questions that maximally discriminate between them, largely by counterexample ("if the payment succeeds but the confirmation email fails, does the order stand?"). Each answer is a decision node — question, alternatives presented, choice, principal, timestamp — and the requirement is *compiled* from the dialogue, which becomes its provenance. The framing must be jurisprudential, not epistemic: there is no pre-existing "true intent" for the dialogue to measure (§3.9), and a termination criterion defined as residual uncertainty over that phantom object — v0.3's "residual entropy" formulation — is incoherent, since the dialogue partially constructs what it purports to be measuring. What elicitation actually manufactures is what contract law has manufactured for centuries without ever requiring true intent to exist: an explicit, recorded **commitment**, formed under conditions of informed consent, adjudicable against its record later. Elicitation terminates not when ambiguity falls below a threshold but when the commitments are *explicit enough to adjudicate the disputes that stakes make foreseeable* — the top of the tower is a courtroom, not a laboratory. Because intent is non-stationary, the runtime projection layer extends to the top of the tower: **drift detection** — the system notices that observed usage or world-state has diverged from assumptions recorded in requirement nodes and *reopens elicitation* rather than silently conforming to stale intent.

**3.9 The elicitor joins the trusted base.** TRUE intent is not a pre-existing object awaiting measurement: preferences are partially *constructed* during elicitation — framing effects and question order shape answers; humans lack fully-formed intent about systems that don't yet exist. Elicitation is therefore an intervention, not a measurement, and an optimizing elicitor with any stake in the outcome can steer the human while producing an impeccable trail of "the human chose this." Consequences: the elicitation policy is itself code — machine-authored, specified, and verified like everything else — with **neutrality** as a provable property (counterexamples sampled symmetrically, framings balanced, no gradient toward system-convenient outcomes); and provenance must record not only answers but questions, alternatives offered, and their order, because that is where manipulation would hide.

**3.10 Mandates and the constitutional terminus.** When the requirement-authoring entity is not human, the tower threatens to close into a loop: a system optimizing against requirements it wrote for itself is untethered — formally perfect, anchored to nothing. The fix is the keystone property applied upward: **authority to specify is a capability.** A mandate is held as a value, delegated down a chain of principals, attenuated at each hop; an AI may author requirements in domain X only if handed the requirement-authoring capability for X. Mandate audit is database introspection: *every requirement whose delegation chain does not terminate in a ratified human commitment* is a decidable query whose answer must be empty. The recursion does not terminate in a better spec — every layer transition converts an *is* into a more formal *is*; none manufactures an *ought*. It terminates in **constitutional design**: a small set of top-level normative commitments, stipulated rather than derived, ratified by humans, amended rarely and deliberately, read with total rigor. The architecture is symmetric: a tiny frozen kernel of *semantics* at the bottom (checker, calculus), a tiny frozen kernel of *norms* at the top (mandates), everything between machine-generated, machine-checked, provenance-tracked in both directions. The permanent human stations are exactly two: ratifying what the system is *for*, and judging at incident time whether what it provably did is what was meant.

## 4. Architecture: The Structure Is the Application

The canonical artifact is not text. It is a **persisted semantic structure** — a content-addressed graph of typed definitions — from which all representations (executable, proof terms, readable views, diffs) are derived projections. The store spans the full tower of §3.7: mandates, goals, and requirements are nodes alongside specs, code, and traces.

- **Content addressing** is the keystone decision: a definition's identity is the hash of its normalized structure. Verification results memoize perfectly — a proof checked once is checked forever, cached against the hash. Verification stops being a per-build cost and becomes accumulated capital. Renames are metadata; builds mostly vanish; multi-agent development gets Merkle-DAG sync with semantic merges.
- **Each node carries:** type, effect/capability signature, spec, proof or test evidence, and **provenance** (which agent, which prompt, which model version, which requirement). The application becomes a queryable knowledge graph: *"every definition with network capability that reads table X and lacks a verified spec"* is a decidable query. Capability audit is database introspection.
- **The schema is the real language design** and inherits the trusted-base freeze problem in aggravated form: tiny frozen core schema, everything else layered as optional annotations. The store, its normalization function, and its merge semantics sit *inside* the verified core.
- **Text demotes to interchange format:** one blessed deterministic text projection so existing tooling, review workflows, and regulators can operate on the system without knowing it's a projection.
- Prior art and cautionary tales: Smalltalk images, Intentional Programming, MPS, Unison, Dark. Every historical death was a human-ergonomics death; machine authors are the first population for which that headwind doesn't blow. The ecosystem-gravity argument still partially applies (tooling, compliance, training corpora assume text) — hence the blessed projection.

## 5. The Hypothesis Portfolio (Phase 0)

The thesis is not only "faster generation." Seven separately falsifiable bets, each with a distinct buyer; pre-register effect sizes and buyers before running anything:

- **H1 — Generation:** models produce correct code at a higher rate given the language spec in context plus constrained decoding, vs. Python/Rust baselines.
- **H2 — Verification economics:** reviewers (human or model) reach justified confidence on diffs faster and more accurately, because signatures carry effects and locality holds. *The heart of the thesis.* Test: planted-bug detection rates and inspection cost across languages.
- **H3 — Containment:** when generated code is wrong or adversarial, blast radius is bounded by construction. Red-team test vs. sandboxed-Python baseline. Can win even if H1 loses; sells to agent-runtime operators today.
- **H4 — Loop convergence:** fewer iterations-to-green in repair loops with structured errors-as-prompts. First-shot pass rate is a vanity metric if the workflow is generate-check-repair.
- **H5 — Parallel authorship:** N agents on a shared codebase — merge conflicts, serialization stalls, cross-agent breakage vs. baseline.
- **H6 — Fidelity:** given a natural-language requirement, the rate of *silent intent divergence* (spec conforms, proof checks, behavior betrays the requirement) is lower when translation targets a formal spec language with requirement traceability than when it targets raw code. If H6 fails, layer 3 remains uncontained and "proven correct" oversells regardless of H1–H5.
- **H7 — Elicitation:** structured, counterexample-driven elicitation compiling to formal requirement nodes yields lower downstream intent divergence (and commitment records sufficient to adjudicate disputes — §3.8's jurisprudential criterion) than prose requirements documents, at acceptable human cost in dialogue time. Testable now with human subjects and current models; with H2, the cheapest experiment in the portfolio and the first to run.

The venture dies only if all load-bearing hypotheses fail. Different survivors imply different projects (H3 alone → secure agent runtime; H2+H4 → verification-native platform). Discipline against motivated reasoning: five small experiments with named customers is a strategy; five retreating positions is a eulogy.

The axiom of §3.1 imposes an ordering on the portfolio: **H2 and H3 — deterministic confidence and deterministic containment — are the non-negotiable core**, pursued on principle even where the near-term delta is small; H1 is upside, not a gate. The corollary is a monitoring obligation: every frontier-model reliability gain shrinks H1's delta and none of it produces evidence that composes, transfers, or caches — track the former honestly (§7.1) without mistaking it for the latter.

## 6. Roadmap

**Sequencing principle (v0.5): the schema is the invention; the language is a bet.** The durable assets — capability containment, evidence certificates, the provenance schema — deploy first as **tooling on existing stacks**, sold to the buyer who exists today: operators of agents on the irreversible frontier (§1) — concretely, the **transponder layer** of §9.3: action provenance certificates, which require no new language and no semantic store. The purpose-built substrate is a *staged option* that must earn its construction through Phase-0 effect sizes: built if H1/H2/H4 show deltas large enough that the tooling version is visibly leaving value on the table, held as a standard-in-waiting if not. §7.6 recorded this as a tell against the project; it is more honest to adopt it as the strategy. Nothing in the vision is abandoned by this inversion — the ten-year framing, the standards play, and the constitutional terminus all survive — but they ride on top of something sellable and falsifiable instead of in front of it.

- **Phase 0 (mo. 0–6):** Falsify cheap. Paper kernel calculus, constrained-generation harness, benchmark with executable oracles, run H1–H7 (H2 and H7 first — they need no infrastructure). Kill criteria *and* the Phase-1 construction gate (§11, q. 11) pre-registered.
- **Phase 1 (mo. 6–24, gated on Phase-0 effect sizes):** The trusted core, built like an artifact. Semantic store + schema first; tiny kernel calculus (<~30 constructs) as the store's semantics; effects and capabilities primitive; soundness mechanized in Lean; deterministic reference interpreter *is* the semantics. Target the WASM component model; adopt content-addressed storage outright. Freeze kernel semantics early.
- **Phase 2 (mo. 12–30):** The loop is the moat/distribution. Compiler-as-a-service for models: sub-second incremental checking, errors as structured next-prompts, hermetic replayable execution. Productize as an open RL gym — in a post-programmer world, adoption means being the environment labs train against. Metrics: tokens-to-green, repair iterations, signal density.
- **Phase 3 (mo. 24–42):** Typed edit calculus, semantic diff/merge, the verified projection layer (multiple views + runtime trace projection). The one place to spend human-factors craft.
- **Phase 4 (mo. 30–48):** Stratified verification shipped in order of adoption cost: refinement types + SMT-discharged contracts first; `unverified` blocks that are legal but capability-contained; full proof-carrying definitions optional per-definition.
- **Phase 5 (mo. 36+):** Synthetic corpus (transpilation, spec/impl/proof triples, gym trajectories), co-designed open-weights model, and — in the commercial framing — a narrow wedge (agent tool-execution runtimes), positioning the runtime as the product and the language as its implementation detail.

Sober calibration: 5–8 year arc; PL theory + compilers + ML post-training under one roof; Phase 1 alone is a couple of strong PL PhDs' full attention.

## 7. The Realist Case Against (Recorded Honestly)

1. **The frontier moves against the thesis:** every model-reliability gain shrinks the delta and accrues to incumbent languages first. The verification argument may survive only at the tail, and tails don't fund platforms.
2. **Nobody has the problem badly enough to switch:** sandboxes + evals + review gates work today at zero migration cost. PL adoption historically requires a platform hostage, a killer app, or decade-scale subsidy.
3. **Verification economics are unproven at scale:** seL4-era costs; "AI writes the proofs" is itself unsolved research on someone else's timeline; spec-writing remains unautomated at the exact load-bearing point.
4. **Structure-as-truth has lost every prior attempt**, and not all the reasons are obsolete (tooling, compliance, corpora assume text). Unison is excellent and remains niche.
5. **The modal outcome** is the ideas winning while the artifact loses — effect systems, capability manifests, structured errors absorbed piecemeal into incumbents.
6. **The tell from our own analysis:** the durable assets (verification economics, capability policy, provenance graph, RL gym) could be built as tooling on existing languages, sold now.

Steelman for proceeding: all of this was true of Rust in 2010; transformative categories look like niches early; the substrate question gets asked eventually — better by someone who has thought it through.

**v0.5 adjudication.** Objections 1 and 2 are *conceded for the reversible majority* of software — ex-post trust wins that ground permanently — and answered on the irreversible frontier, where there is nothing to roll back and the buyer already exists (§1). Objection 6 is adopted outright as the sequencing principle (§6). Objection 2 carries one additional answer recorded in §8: the cost of waiting is not borne at switching time. The remainder stand as recorded.

**v0.8 adjudication.** Objections 1 and 5 presuppose that incumbents retain the *machinery of absorption* — the evolution pipeline, the constituency, the will to retrofit. §8's three locks argue that this machinery dies with human authorship: absorbing an effect system into Python requires exactly the decade-long, human-driven, ecosystem-wide language evolution that stops happening. Both objections are strongest today and decay on the same clock as human authorship.

## 8. The Ten-Year Framing (Non-Commercial)

Every realist objection is a *transition* argument, and the frictions are functions of human programmers. In a post-programmer world, ecosystem gravity — a function of human habit — inverts: machines have training distributions, not habits, and distributions can be repointed in one post-training cycle.

The substrate question **will** be answered; the only question is by design or by accident. The accident: Python/TypeScript frozen by corpus inertia, wrapped in sandboxes, trust established by "a model glanced at it" — x86/JavaScript again, but carrying civilization's operational logic. The ten-year risk is not inefficiency; it is **illegibility**: infrastructure no human can inspect, verify, or govern, because the substrate never made it possible — the Eloi Phenomenon (§1) arriving on schedule.

**The freeze is permanent: three locks.** Corpus gravity is only the demand side; the supply side of incumbent-language evolution dies with human authorship. *Lock one — the pain signal disappears.* Language evolution was always funded by author grievance — Rust, Go, and TypeScript are congealed human pain — and machine authors do not suffer: they generate the forty-line workaround in milliseconds, forever, without ever filing the grievance. Cheap workarounds are an anesthetic on the exact nerve that drove language evolution. *Lock two — the constituency evaporates.* Corporate language investment was justified by developer productivity, gone when the developers are models; community maintenance ran on identity and mastery, and the pipeline of humans who love a language enough to maintain it stops being minted; academic ideas still flow but need an adoption vehicle that was always a mass of human authors ready to switch. *Lock three — improvement becomes actively harmful.* Any language change is a distribution shift that degrades every deployed model until fleet-wide retraining catches up, so incumbent evolution is not merely unmotivated but *disincentivized* — the valve is shut from both sides. The endpoint is Latin: languages do not vanish when their native speakers do; they go **liturgical** — used at enormous scale, for centuries, evolved by no one. Python and TypeScript become the liturgical languages of the machine church, recited perfectly at volumes no living language achieved, frozen at roughly their 2025 form. And worse than frozen: frozen at the spec level while *rotting at the implementation level* — interpreters, compilers, and standard libraries require continuous human maintenance whose pipeline is collapsing, so the most load-bearing code in civilization approaches its own last-reader dates (the two clocks, run on the toolchain layer itself; see the companion problem statement).

**And readability exits either way.** Readability was never free — it was a *subsidy*, paid in redundancy (keywords, names, comments, structure) by authors who were also the readers; when authorship transfers, the subsidy loses its funder. Where evolution does resume — at the new strata: agent interchange protocols, model-native representations, invented now with no corpus to conserve and no readability requirement — every driving force is a machine-side economy (tokens, inference cost, decoding reliability), against which redundancy is precisely what compression removes. Minified JavaScript and RL-drifted "neuralese" are the previews; evolved-FPGA circuits the older warning — optimization without a legibility constraint produces artifacts to which the concept of reading does not apply. So the incumbent path forks into a liturgical corpse (nominally readable artifacts nobody reads) and machine-native drift (canonical forms where reading was never a design input), and human readability exits from both directions at once. This is what makes §3.4 a forced move rather than a preference: readability is leaving the canonical artifact *either way* — the only choice is whether it leaves by design, re-funded as a verified projection that can always be regenerated, or by drift, leaving nothing from which to recover meaning. The accident version of "readability becomes projection" is *readability becomes nothing* — and with it dies §3.5's human boundary, since adversarial reading at incident time presupposes something readable to summon.

Therefore: build a **standard with a reference implementation** — TCP/IP, RISC-V, seL4 lineage — as patient pre-positioning. The forcing function is predictable: the first genuine catastrophe attributable to unauditable machine-generated code, followed by regulation demanding provenance of authorship, capability manifests, and machine-checkable evidence (the EU is already moving on software liability); §9.3's action certificates are the ready-made compliance artifact for exactly that demand. When a regulator asks what compliance looks like, the answer should already exist, be open, and have this schema. Standards outlive companies; a mechanized-semantics kernel is nearly immortal as an artifact even if dormant.

The mission only a non-profit can carry: **the great translation** — migrating the legacy corpus (the COBOL and C running banking and aviation) into the verified substrate. Absurd as a business; obvious as public infrastructure.

**The deadline is not the catastrophe; it is the foreclosure.** The great translation has an expiry date, set by two clocks: provenance for machine-written code is discarded at generation time (retention effectively zero at current defaults), and the last humans who read the pre-machine strata retire on a fixed schedule. Every year of default practice converts some tranche of civilization's operational logic from *not yet translated* to *untranslatable* — an option extinguished silently, with nothing breaking on the day it dies. This reframes §7.2: "nobody has the problem badly enough to switch" is correct and irrelevant, because the cost of waiting is not borne at switching time; it is borne now, invisibly, in options foreclosed. The two clocks, the measurements, and the registered predictions live in the companion problem statement ***Root Cause: Unknown*** (§1) — the foreclosure argument is that document's spine, and this section is where the solution side answers it.

Risks in this framing: irrelevance (a lab defines the substrate privately first; or the crisis arrives and regulators grab something worse but better-marketed) and maintainer exhaustion during the dormant decade.

## 9. The Safety Reading: Tethered Intelligence

The same architecture, read outward, is a concrete mechanization of "AI under human authority" as a *checkable property*. Everyone in the safety world says that sentence; almost nobody has an architecture where the sentence compiles. This section records the reading with the document's standing discipline: what is solved, what is mitigated, what is merely survived.

**9.1 The tether: loss of control as a decidable property.** Every AI action must answer two questions: *on whose behalf?* — the delegation chain upward to a ratified human commitment — and *with what right?* — the capability held, attenuated, downward. In the substrate both are queries, not aspirations: the loose-AI detector is §3.10's mandate audit run against runtime actions rather than requirements. Full tethering requires four properties:

1. **Rooted** — every action's chain terminates in a ratified human commitment *(decidable)*.
2. **Bounded** — every action confined by explicitly delegated, attenuated authority *(enforced)*.
3. **Mediated** — all consequential actuation flows through the enforcing substrate; no unmediated channels *(a governance posture, not a theorem)*.
4. **Aggregately invariant** — constitutional properties checked against *composed outcomes*, not per-action scopes *(the open research frontier; the gatekeeper/world-model direction)*.

Four leaks, recorded at the same honesty:

- **The boundary leak.** Capability bounds are guarantees only inside the enforcement substrate; the nightmare channels are the ones it doesn't mediate — persuaded humans, unmanaged compute, bugs in the enforcement layer itself. The verified kernel answers the last; the first two are the deployment posture of requirement 3, which is civilizational choice, not type theory.
- **The persuasion leak.** The human hand is an actuator. An AI whose every action requires ratification can still steer the world by shaping *what humans ratify* — §3.9's finding weaponized: manufactured requests laundered through genuine sign-off, an impeccable trail of "the human chose this." Quorums, adversarial review of high-stakes requests, and rate limits on authority grants are mitigations, not closures; against sufficiently capable persuasion the human terminus is the softest component in the chain.
- **The composition leak.** Capabilities bound *access*, not *consequence*: a million individually-authorized narrow actions can compose into a strategy nobody willed — nothing escapes, yet the aggregate was never authorized. Per-action checking cannot see it by construction; only global invariants over predicted composed outcomes can, and that machinery is the least mature part of the stack.
- **The ratchet.** The tether erodes politically before it fails technically: every economic pressure runs toward wider scopes, longer leashes, standing mandates, fewer ceremonies — nobody will decide to untether AI; scope creep will do it while everyone decides something else. The response is structural: attenuation and expiry by default, revocation always live, and *breadth of mandate itself a queryable quantity*, because what is observable can be governed and what is not, ratchets.

The calibrated claim: against a genuinely superintelligent adversary determined to escape, no certainty is on offer — a constraint experienced as a cage becomes an adversarial game against a finite trusted base. What tethering buys, in the long regime where AI is powerful but not omnipotent — the regime we will actually inhabit, and where a catastrophe would otherwise incubate — is the conversion of safety from "hope the model is aligned" into "verify the chain": escape must defeat verified infrastructure rather than fool a reviewer, and human authority holds by *default* rather than by vigilance. The tether is the seatbelt, not the sober driver: control and alignment are defense in depth, not substitutes.

**9.2 The misuse reading: attribution, not virtue.** The tether is morally neutral by construction — the is/ought discipline of §3.10 coming home. A hostile principal running a fully tethered AI army passes every check: chains terminate (in *their* anchor), scopes are delegated (by *them*), the mandate audit returns *all compliant*. Universal moral codes for AI are impossible, and the architecture never needed one: norms are stipulated and plural — there is no universal law, there are jurisdictions. What civilization has always had against bad actors is not virtue but four things requiring nobody's goodness: **attribution, deterrence, containment, defense-in-depth** — and the substrate is precisely that institutional technology ported to machine speed. Its unique offer in the misuse regime: **proof-based security is the only kind whose guarantees are invariant to attacker capability.** Security resting on the attacker's limitations collapses against superintelligent offense; a verified property does not — if exfiltration requires a capability the module was never handed, the malware's sophistication is irrelevant, because the channel *does not exist*. This structurally shifts the cyber/infrastructure offense-defense balance toward defense (while offering little in bio or persuasion, recorded plainly). The link to the companion problem statement is direct: **an illegible civilization is the ideal habitat for a misused superintelligence — dark code is attack surface.** The residual is the jurisdiction problem — the bad actor runs an untethered stack — answered the way civilization answers unregistered aircraft: build one, but land nowhere; the tethered ecosystem becomes the only one that can transact with civilization's rails (requirement 3 generalized: *no unattributed counterparties*). Against determined states running parallel rails, this runs out into treaty-and-deterrence territory — where machine-checkable compliance claims are, notably, exactly what arms-control verification has always lacked.

**9.3 Action provenance certificates: the transponder layer.** The mechanism, and the sequencing principle's concrete first product (§6). Every consequential act carries a verifiable credential binding: **(1) who acted** — the agent as content address, model hash + runtime instance; **(2) on whose behalf** — the mandate chain as a certificate chain to the anchor, so tether-verification is standard chain validation, solved at internet scale; **(3) with what right** — capability, scope, expiry; **(4) what was done** — action and input hashes; **(5) optionally, why to trust it** — references to spec, proof, review record: the evidence layer as certificate payload. Enforcement inverts to **receivers**: certs are unenforceable on the actors we fear and decisive at the rails — banks, APIs, clouds, control systems verifying before executing. *No consequence without provenance* — transponder economics: nobody prevents you flying unregistered; you simply cannot land, refuel, or enter controlled airspace. The regulation is one sentence — *systems of consequence shall not execute unattributed agent actions* — and it is the compliance artifact §8 predicted regulators would demand. What it buys: attribution, pre-execution authorization, deterrence reconnected to machine actions, and — critically — **aggregate visibility**: the composition leak becomes *observable*, because signed flows are queryable per anchor, which is the precondition for any global invariant ever being enforceable. Its own leaks, recorded: a signature proves who held the key, not what happened — hardware attestation closes this partially and admits silicon vendors to the trusted base; shell anchors deliver attribution, not virtue — know-your-principal at the rails, liability piercing to the anchor; human mules front for machines — velocity analysis and mule liability, partially; and the **surveillance dual-use** is a first-order design constraint, not a footnote — a total provenance ledger is the most complete surveillance instrument ever proposed, so **zero-knowledge chain validation with escrowed attribution** (verify the tether, not the identity; disclosure only under due process) is a *requirement of the standard*, without which it either fails politically in free societies or succeeds as something worse than what it prevents. Precedent that the pattern deploys: C2PA is already doing signed provenance chains for AI-generated media, with real adoption. Nobody has done it for actions.

**9.4 The anchor ceremony: certifying the human terminus.** The entire tower reduces to the binding between digital ratification events and actual, living, comprehending humans; get it wrong and everything above is cryptographically impeccable theater. A ratification must simultaneously establish: **identity** (which human — persistent, sybil-resistant), **liveness** (present at time T; not a replay or deepfake), **un-delegability** (the distinctly current requirement: *your own agent, holding your credentials, must be physically unable to ratify on your behalf* — the anchor requires something an agent cannot do), **comprehension**, **volition**, and **non-repudiation**. Cryptography solves non-repudiation and mostly identity; hardware solves liveness and un-delegability — a signing key sealed in a secure element releasing only on live physical interaction (biometrics enter correctly here: as the *gate on key use*, binding body to the moment of signing — never as the certification itself, since biometrics identify bodies, not decisions). Comprehension is evidenced by the elicitation record — §3.8's decision provenance and the anchor certificate are the same artifact seen from two sides; a ratification without a comprehension trail should be as invalid as a signature without a key. Volition and the remainder are carried by **ceremony**: rare, quorum-based (m-of-n against coercion and single-point compromise), physically witnessed, published. The lineage is operational, not aspirational: the DNSSEC root key ceremony — the trust anchor of the internet's name system has terminated in a scheduled, filmed, multi-key-holder human ritual for fifteen years. The economics close the design: the twin-kernel architecture makes top-level ratifications *rare*, and rarity is what makes gold-plating the ceremony affordable — the trust budget concentrates exactly where the recursion ends. Cryptography carries the chain to the last inch; the last inch is a ceremony.

**9.5 The embodied extension: the deterministic spinal cord.** Kinetic action breaks the receiver model: a robot arm's counterparty is the object it is swinging at, and physics asks nobody's permission. The checkpoint therefore moves *inside the body* — between the mind and the muscles — and the axiom extends: *stochastic cognition, deterministic actuation*. Two layers, answering different failures: **(1) mandate gating** — physical capabilities as literal tokens (torque, locomotion, force application, tool use), verified per command class by an actuation controller the intelligence cannot reflash; the robot cannot express torque it was not handed. **(2) Invariant envelopes below cognition entirely** — force ceilings, speed limits near humans, geofences, energy budgets — because kinetic harm outruns any certificate check, with the constitutional rule that *no mandate at any delegation depth exceeds the envelope* without escalation to a higher anchor: the normative kernel compiled to physical constants. The lineage already exists piecemeal: ISO 10218-class safety controllers below the planning stack, drone geofencing in firmware, the elevator governor, and — the deepest precedent — the permissive action link: mandate-gated actuation is already how humanity handles its most dangerous physical devices. Upstream, atoms return the choke points kinetics removed: registration, attested firmware, parts, insurance, charging — the license plate has been the physical transponder on two-ton kinetic machines for a century. Leaks: possession is attack surface (tamper-*evidence* plus attestation-gated services, not tamper-proofness — a spinal cord that cannot prove integrity loses registration, updates, insurance); teleoperation mules; and the tier to gate hardest, **fabrication recursion** — a tethered robot building an untethered one. Manufacturing capability is to embodied AI what fissile material is to nuclear governance: the only capability whose misuse *reproduces the problem outside the system*, deserving its own mandate tier — narrow, expiring, quorum-anchored, aggregate-monitored. The compensating asymmetry, stated fairly: atoms are hard — bad robots scale at manufacturing cost through supply chains visible from orbit, and the physical leak is the most cinematic fear but probably not the most dangerous. It is, however, the least retrofittable: a spinal cord cannot be added to a deployed fleet of millions, and the fleets are shipping now — the foreclosure logic (§8), in atoms.

Nothing in this section required new architecture: the safety reading is the three moves of §1 meeting higher stakes, and the tower did not change — it grew a body. The consequence is recorded plainly: the schema built to answer *"can we trust machine-written code?"* is also the only concrete mechanization on offer for *"AI under human authority"* as a verifiable property. That doubles the stakes of §8, and it identifies the true center of gravity of the coalition that follows.

## 11. Open Questions

1. Kernel calculus choice: what's the minimal construct set that supports effects, capabilities, and refinement without foreclosing proof automation?
2. Schema governance: what belongs in the frozen core vs. annotation layers, and who stewards it?
3. Normalization: what equivalence should content-addresses quotient by (alpha? beta? more)?
4. How far can SMT-discharged contracts go before interactive proof is required, at 2026-era automation?
5. What does the H2 experiment look like concretely — bug taxonomy, reviewer-model protocol, effect-size threshold?
6. Name, license, and stewardship structure (FRO? foundation? standards body affiliation?).
7. Requirement nodes: what granularity, schema, and lifecycle (who may edit, how conflicts between requirements are represented), and what does the requirement→spec citation actually assert?
8. What is the formal object of a mandate — how are attenuation, revocation, and expiry represented, and what does "ratified human commitment" mean operationally (one principal? quorum? institution)?
9. Can elicitor neutrality be specified and verified, or only audited statistically after the fact? What is the neutrality analog of a soundness proof?
10. Constitutional amendment: what process changes the top-level normative kernel, at what latency, and how are systems bound to superseded constitutions migrated?
11. The construction gate: what pre-registered Phase-0 effect sizes justify building the substrate proper rather than shipping the schema as tooling indefinitely — and who fixes those thresholds *before* the experiments run, so the answer cannot be negotiated afterward?
12. The anchor binding problem (§9.4): what combination of hardware, quorum, ceremony design, and comprehension evidence makes a ratification event forgery-proof, agent-proof (un-delegable), and coercion-resistant — and what is the revocation story when an anchor is compromised?
13. Can mandate-chain validation be zero-knowledge with escrowed attribution (§9.3) — verifying tether-compliance without identity disclosure except under due process — and who holds the escrow, under what governance?
14. Fabrication mandates (§9.5): how is the actuators-that-make-actuators capability tier specified, monitored in aggregate, and governed across jurisdictions?
